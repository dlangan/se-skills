# Connected Vision — Voice Rules

**Purpose:** Style and voice guide for all Connected Vision artifacts — taglines, pillar headlines, pillar descriptions, and outcome nodes. Load this file whenever drafting or reviewing any CV element. Read by `se-connected-vision` and referenced by `se-northstar-pov` (Step 14) for theme wording.

---

## Tagline

**Rule #1: Take it from the customer's website first.** Hero text, mission statement, section headers, CEO quotes — if the language is already theirs, use it verbatim or as close as possible. Only craft something original if no usable source exists on their site.

**Rule #2: Shortest possible.** 2–5 words is the target. Every extra word is a failure of editing.

**Rule #3: Tension or transformation implied — never explained.** The reader fills in the stakes themselves.

**Rule #4: No rhyme. No pun** unless the brand naturally plays with its own product or name (e.g., a packaging company punning on its own name in the tagline — no one else earns that).

**Rule #5: Never generic.** If it could appear on any other customer's slide unchanged, start over.

**Proven structures (patterns observed across real engagements — company identities anonymized):**
- **Heritage anchor:** "Built Useful, Built to Last" (Aerospace/Manufacturing account) — long history, durability is the identity
- **Industry metaphor:** "Where Water Meets Mettle" (Marine/Shipbuilding account) — double meaning, industry-native
- **Compression:** "One Driving Force" (Automotive Manufacturing account) — 3 words, maximum weight
- **Tension:** "Doing It Right This Time" (Construction Technology account) — self-aware, implies prior struggle
- **Operational KPI:** "100% On Time, Error Free, Exceptional Fill Rates" (Industrial Distribution account) — their own performance standard as aspiration

## Pillar Headlines

**Rule #1: Verb-first or provocative noun phrase.** Never a passive description.

**Rule #2: Short and slightly uncomfortable.** The best ones make the reader pause:
- "Experts Over Admin" (Energy Distribution account) — tension in 3 words
- "Jobless Expansion" (Automotive Manufacturing account) — 2 words, provocative
- "Close the Action Gap" (Field Operations/Energy Tech account) — verb + stakes
- "Let Customers Buy" (Industrial Manufacturing account) — imperative, dead simple
- "Industrializing the Basics" (Construction Technology account) — reframes mundane work as strategic choice

**Rule #3: "One X" when unification is the core pain.** "One Brain", "One Truth", "One Network", "One Standard" — proven for fragmented-data or multi-entity situations.

**Rule #4: Must be specific to THIS customer.** If you can swap it onto another slide without changing a word, it's not done.

**Rule #5: Industry language welcome.** "Steel-Sharp Intelligence" (Metals/Steel Distribution account), "Regionally Strong, Agentic Driven" (Regional Manufacturing account), "Proactive Fleet Stewardship" (Aerospace/Fleet Services account) — their world's vocabulary over ours.

---

## Pillar Descriptions (body copy)

**Rule #1: Open with the mechanism** — what specifically changes, not why it matters.

**Rule #2: Include one concrete detail** — a number, a named system, a named role, a specific pain. "Tens of thousands of dormant leads." "The 'swivel-chair tax'." "Millions in spare parts revenue visible to more than one person's spreadsheet." (Use real figures from account research when available — round or range them if the exact number is commercially sensitive.)

**Rule #3: No Salesforce product names in the first sentence.** Function first, then capability.

---

## Outcome Nodes (8 right-side labels)

**Rule #1: Noun phrases only, not sentences.** "Sales Capacity Reclaimed." "Frictionless Partner Experience." "Audit-Ready Compliance."

**Rule #2: 2–4 words.** Follows BVS KPI format.

**Rule #3: Results, not restatements.** Don't echo the pillar headline word-for-word.

**Rule #4: Reflect the combined effect of all 3 pillars** — not 2-3 per pillar mechanically.

---

## The Final Test

Read the whole slide out loud. If it could belong to any company in this industry, start over. If it could only belong to *this* customer, you're done.
